House #1 · Chelmsford, MA · Nov 13, 2026 · MLS #73599405

The Ranch That Wanted to Be a Colonial

3 Balsam Dr, Chelmsford — $699,900. A lovely 1959 ranch with one small problem: we wanted it to be a completely different house.

Rejected

The listing, as the agent tells it

Corner lot on a quiet cul-de-sac. Four bedrooms (fine print: the fourth is in the basement), two full baths, a fenced yard, a screened porch, a grilling deck with its own gas line, and a genuinely oversized 3-car garage. Gleaming hardwoods, a brick fireplace, mini-splits, updated windows. One storey of very pleasant 1959 ranch, 1,266 sq ft above grade plus about 500 finished in the basement, on 0.41 acres. Public sewer, public water.

All true, and all charming. Also: $699,900 is $396 per sq ft on the total finished space, and Zillow's own estimate for the place is $630,900. We were being asked to pay near-finished money for a starting point. But we had a plan, and the plan had a second floor in it.

The recipe we had in mind: Second-Storey Ranch

Serves: 5–6 bedroomsPrep: 6–12 monthsCook time: one New England winter, ideally not yoursCost per serving: ~$550,000

Ingredients: 1 ranch (1959, load-bearing capacity unknown) · 1 roof, removed · ~1,266 sq ft of new framing · 1 staircase (takes 80–100 sq ft from downstairs — nowhere is free) · 1 new bathroom, non-negotiable · structural engineer, architect, surveyor, permits to taste.

Method: Remove roof. Discover what the foundation thinks about all this. Build up. Re-roof. Move out for the middle part. Serve hot, roughly a year later.

Step 1: Would the town even allow it?

Surprisingly, yes — probably. The house sits in Chelmsford's RB district, which allows up to 3 storeys and 35 ft of height, 15% building coverage, and a floor-area ratio of 0.20. Building straight up inside the existing footprint:

The lot itself is nonconforming — RB wants 40,000 sq ft and this parcel has 17,672 — but Chelmsford's §195-8E was written for exactly this: a single-family home on an undersized lot can be extended by right with a Building Commissioner determination, provided setbacks, coverage, height and FAR all comply. If one doesn't, the fallback is a ZBA special permit under the friendly "not substantially more detrimental" standard. Zoning: not the villain of this story.

The villain is the foundation. A 1959 ranch was engineered to hold up one floor and a roof, not a second storey. Whether it can — or what $5k–$30k of reinforcement makes it able to — is a structural engineer's question, and nobody can answer it from a listing. That unknown sits underneath every number that follows.

It isn't. 11 Sanford is a 2004 purpose-built Colonial with 3,048 sq ft above grade plus 1,323 sq ft of finished basement — over 4,000 sq ft of living space and 3 baths. Why it was rejected

Not because the town would stop us — it mostly wouldn't. Not because the house is bad — it isn't. It was rejected because the plan only ever worked in the one currency that doesn't spend: hope that a renovated ranch would be valued like a Colonial. At $699,900 in and ~$550,000 of building, we'd own a ~$1.03M house for ~$1.28M, and the gap does not close at any plausible negotiated price.

The exception, stated fairly: as a 10+ year family home, where the return is the living in it and Chelmsford's ~5%/yr appreciation has half a decade to heal the wound, this could still be somebody's right answer. It is not the numbers' answer, and this blog follows the numbers.

Lesson filed for House #2: when the plan requires the house to become a different house, price the finished product against houses that were born that way — not against the dream.
