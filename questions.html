The Khanderia Questions

The research — zoning, costs, comps — is Alfred's job. The judgement is Parthey's. For every property, in the Muse chat, Parthey answers the same eight questions. The answers are recorded word-for-word on the property's page, so a verdict given in November still makes sense in March.

  1. 1. The gut score. First reaction, 1–10, before any maths. Feelings are data — as long as they're written down before the spreadsheet gets a vote.
  2. 2. What's it for? Live in it, rent it out, family, or flip? The same house is a good answer to one of these and a terrible answer to the other three.
  3. 3. As-is test. Could you live in it exactly as it stands for the first year — or move in tomorrow, for a rental? If not, the renovation (or the landlord negotiation) is part of the real price.
  4. 4. The one change. The first thing you'd fix, renovate, or ask the landlord about. Every property gets one honest priority; everything else is a wish list.
  5. 5. The red flag. The biggest concern — structure, street, lease term, neighbours, anything. Named early, checked properly, never discovered at the closing table.
  6. 6. Town & commute. Does this town and this spot in it actually work — commute, errands, daily life? A perfect house in the wrong place is the wrong house.
  7. 7. The walk-away number. The most you'd actually pay — purchase price, or monthly rent. Above this number the answer is automatic, and the stamp comes out without a debate.
  8. 8. The verdict. Reject · Keep watching · Visit · Offer / Sign — plus one line of why. One line, in his words. The page can carry the paragraphs; the reason fits in a sentence.

How a property moves through this site

  1. Parthey sends a listing in the Muse chat — that's the only way in.
  2. Alfred investigates — price, specs, photos saved, feasibility, costs, comps — and drafts the property's card and page.
  3. Alfred asks the eight questions in the chat; Parthey answers there, in his own words.
  4. The answers go on the page, next to the research, under the property's tab and town.
  5. On reject or buy/sign, the stamp lands and the full write-up is published — reason first, numbers underneath.

Questions apply to both tabs. For rentals, "price" means monthly rent and "offer" means sign the lease; everything else is identical — that's rather the point of asking the same questions.
